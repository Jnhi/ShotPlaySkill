# Example Style Bible — the 7-Step Method, Fully Worked ("Snowblade Inn")

> **This is a copy-my-homework file.** File 11 teaches the 7-step method; this file walks all seven steps on a fictional project so you can see what each answer looks like and how specific it must be. For a new project, copy this structure and replace every cell with your own answers.
> Fictional project: wuxia short drama **"Snowblade Inn"** — a lone inn in a blizzard; the meek innkeeper is a blade master in hiding; seven bounty crews walk in over one night. Vertical 9:16, 60 episodes.

---

## Step 0 · Domain & Mode Declaration (before everything)

```
Render domain = A live-action (why: wuxia texture feeds on real materials and physical
        light; fights feed on physical weight. Anime-flavored project → domain B,
        file 23; 3D-blockbuster flavor → domain C, file 24)
Medium mode = short drama (9:16 vertical, full hook economics, file 04)
Sound = three tracks (dialogue/SFX/ambience), no BGM by default
Style-block declaration (paste into every prompt): "live-action cinematic realism,
        physically-grounded light" + 9:16 vertical composition
Negative-prompt domain pack (paste into every prompt): cartoon look, plastic skin,
        studio-portrait lighting, uniform directionless lighting, foot sliding,
        horizontal composition, background music, soundtrack
```

## Step 1 · The Lifeline Sentence (one line that locks the whole look; must contain opposition)

> **"Snow cold to the bone, fire warm to the heart; until the blade leaves its sheath, the inn is home."**

Self-check: delete the title and read it — can you guess the show? Snow + hearth + blade + inn: yes. ✅
(Counter-example: "cinematic wuxia epic, beautiful visuals" — true of anything = locks nothing. Rejected.)

## Step 2 · Primary Root (one traditional art form as visual DNA; "cinematic" is not a root)

**Northern Song snow-forest landscape painting (the Fan Kuan lineage).**
Why: the inn is "one point of human warmth" and only works when wrapped in "a world of cold" — the genre's tiny-figure-vast-nature composition and dry-brush texture are that relationship, ready-made. Three landings:
1. All exteriors: figures ≤1/5 of frame, snowfield and bare trees ≥4/5 (the source of pressure and loneliness)
2. Material baseline: dry-brush texture → aged wood, coarse hemp, rusted iron, chapped hands; nothing smooth or new
3. Title cards / transitions: ink-silhouette bare branches

## Step 3 · Secondary Roots (1–2, each rules ONE zone, no trespassing)

- **Song-dynasty teahouse genre painting → interiors only**: set-dressing logic (benches, coarse ceramics, cloth banners, the stove); the legal source of warmth. Trespass ban: exteriors never borrow the teahouse warmth.
- **Tang blade catalogs → weapons and fight frames only**: blade geometry, sheath cord wrap, draw angles. Trespass ban: in daily-life scenes the blade stays sheathed in the background.

## Step 4 · Color Recipe (named colors + hex, locked project-wide)

| Role | Color | Hex | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base · cold | indigo night | #2B4055 | snow-night / exterior shadow (snow's dark side is indigo, never grey) |
| Base · snow | moon white | #D8E4E8 | snow highlights / skylight |
| Warm anchor | hearth orange | #C96F33 | stove fire / lanterns / braziers — **at most ONE warm anchor per frame, it owns the eye** |
| Mid tone | aged-wood brown | #6B4F35 | inn timber / furniture / counter |
| Exclusive · killing | frost-edge steel | #9FB8BD | ONLY the reflection on a drawn blade; banned in daily scenes |

- Structure: indigo + wood brown ≈ 80% (low-saturation base) + single hearth-orange point + frost-steel exclusive = cold-base-warm-point formula
- Banned: candy saturation, modern neon, bright red (blood exists only as darkness: black stains on snow)
- Color arc: EP01-10 hearth orange lives only indoors → EP11 identity exposed, frost steel enters the inn for the first time → finale, the hearth goes out, full-frame indigo (home is gone)

## Step 5 · Differentiating Signature (the one X competitors cannot take)

**"Warm anchor extinguished = death grammar"**: the show never films a kill — it films light. Every death, the frame's warm anchor dies in the same beat (a lantern splashed dark / a brazier kicked over / stove fire snuffed by wind). By episode 3 the audience has learned the language: when the fire goes out, someone is gone.
(Signature test: explainable in one sentence + executable all show + meaningless if stolen without your story.)

## Step 6 · Zone Application Table

| Zone | Scenes | Root translation | Color | Light baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold zone | snowfield / road / woods | snow-forest painting, tiny figure vast nature | indigo + moon white | overcast skylight, ratio 4:1, visible breath mandatory |
| Hearth zone | inn hall / kitchen | teahouse genre painting | wood brown + hearth anchor | stove & lantern motivated light, ratio 6:1 |
| Killing zone | blade-draw / duel frames | Tang blade catalogs | frost steel exclusive | single-side hard light, ratio 10:1, shadows swallow everything |

## Step 7 · Verdicts (yes/no answerable)

- Every scene ≥1 poster-grade frame; nothing skippable in the first 3 seconds
- Warm anchors ≤1 per frame; frost steel only on drawn blades; "anchor extinguished" count = death count, audited exactly
- Pull any 3 frames, cover the subtitles: same show?
- Mute any scene: does cold-vs-warm alone tell you who is safe and who is about to die?
