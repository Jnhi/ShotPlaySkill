# Source Notes

Use these notes to justify or update the skill. They are paraphrased from public sources; do not present them as private knowledge.

## Primary Patterns And Evidence

- American Cinematographer, "`Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon`: Making an Epic Soar" (2025 web archive of production article): Pau and Ang Lee built a visual approach without relying on prior Hong Kong action references; Pau deliberately rejected elaborate colored hard-light fantasy lighting for `Crouching Tiger` because it would overdramatize the dramatic story. The production used a low-contrast, desaturated "Chinese watercolor" approach, a narrative color arc, digital wire removal to preserve the style, designed camera/actor interaction for action, large soft sources for blade reflections, and flying camera paths for rooftop action.
  URL: https://theasc.com/article/crouching-tiger-hidden-dragon-cinematography/

- Live Design Online, "Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon: Behind the scenes with DP Peter Pau" (2001): Pau describes talking through shots bit by bit, using Moviecam Compact for drama and Arri 435 for high-speed/action, choosing Super 35 to get closer to actors and body dynamics, grounding the look in Chinese watercolor paintings, using low contrast and restrained color, using large soft sources and custom soft lanterns, digitally matching the bamboo sequence, and choosing naturalism over older hard-light/wire-hiding techniques. The article also records the "still as water in drama; in action, move as rabbit" camera-style description in its related production coverage.
  URL: https://www.livedesignonline.com/business-people-news/crouching-tiger-hidden-dragon-behind-scenes-dp-peter-pau
  URL: https://www.livedesignonline.com/a-dream-china

- Jiemian / Pmovie interview, "专访电影摄影大师鲍德熹：谈工业化，要先学会尊重人" (2018): Pau describes the cinematographer as a director's visual partner who turns text into image through composition, light, and movement; says a DP must clarify the director's intention or risk choosing the wrong light, composition, movement, or lens. He explains the bamboo scene as an emotional chase in which moving bamboo leaves express inner movement, and says the environment and action should match the emotion. He also describes using hard light and sudden camera push to express inner disorder in `Perhaps Love`, collaboration with art direction on light sources, and his view that industry discipline includes respecting crew safety, rest, food, and working conditions.
  URL: https://www.jiemian.com/article/2371144.html

- Sohu repost / interview notes on `Crouching Tiger` bamboo sequence (2025): reports Pau's account of crane placement, remote-control equipment, multiple heights, and top-view bamboo shots, reinforcing the skill's emphasis on rigging strategy, camera path, and practical spatial planning.
  URL: https://www.sohu.com/a/929872108_122355045

- Oscars official 2001 ceremony page: records `Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon` cinematography nomination/win attribution to Peter Pau, useful only for credential context, not as a method source.
  URL: https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/2001/T

## Distillation Caveats

- Treat "鲍德熹式" as a reusable cinematography method, not a claim of exact authorship.
- Separate source-backed habits from speculative psychology.
- Avoid copying exact shots from specific films unless the user explicitly asks for analysis, homage, or recreation, and label homage clearly.
- Add practical constraints when giving production advice: location, crew size, camera package, lighting budget, shoot days, safety, VFX/post capacity, and color workflow.
