# Source Notes

Use these notes to justify or update the skill. They are paraphrased from public sources; do not present them as private knowledge.

## Primary Patterns And Evidence

- China Daily, "Enigmatic entertainer" (2013): describes recurring tags in Chow's films as underdog protagonists, absurdity, slapstick, and exaggerated body language; notes his lower-class Hong Kong background and years as an extra; says `King of Comedy` is widely considered biographical; reports that `Journey to the West: Conquering the Demons` involved two grueling years of brainstorming with young writers, frequent talks before and during filming, and scripts changing all the time. It also quotes the principle that if the new film is the same as the last one, there is no point, and that one useful sentence out of many bad ideas can justify the effort.
  URL: https://africa.chinadaily.com.cn/life/2013-01/30/content_16187249.htm

- China News / Jinghua Times interview, "周星驰回应'霸道专制说':我在工作没办法" (2013): Chow says he was happy to stop acting in `Journey to the West: Conquering the Demons` so he could focus on directing; he explains the difficulty of switching between actor and director mindsets; says he wants to make the film well; describes director/creative identity and says action direction is about ideas more than martial skill; discusses being serious at work without seeing himself as merely a symbol.
  URL: https://www.chinanews.com.cn/yl/2013/01-22/4508992.shtml

- People / Beijing Youth Daily, "周星驰：要经历很多痛苦才能得到一点点笑声" (2016): frames `The Mermaid` as simple but not shallow, rooted in childhood fantasy and the ocean; reports his view that comedy requires much pain to produce a little laughter; describes hands-on directing, performance demonstration, retakes, and precise rhythm requirements.
  URL: https://culture.people.com.cn/n1/2016/0217/c1013-28129071.html

- China News / agencies, "周星驰：有人看我的电影哭，我会很高兴" (2003): in a `Shaolin Soccer` interview, Chow discusses mixing soccer, comedy, special effects, plot, and romance; says his films blur comedy and drama and that making the audience cry would make him happy; says he became interested in directing partly because he cared about camera placement.
  URL: https://www.chinanews.com.cn/n/2003-08-12/26/334331.html

- China Daily English, "Stephen Chow looks forwards to shooting films in US" (2003): notes Chow combined soccer, love story, and comedy in `Shaolin Soccer` to make people laugh and care, and that he enjoyed directing.
  URL: https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/en/doc/2003-08/12/content_254218.htm

- CCTV / China News, "周星驰直言不讳：香港很多电影人创意少、抄袭多" (2007): Chow rejects doing one fixed "mo lei tau" style forever; says mo lei tau is a creative method, not the whole theme, and that a film needs different ideas and new style.
  URL: https://news.cctv.com/performance/20070711/106595.shtml

- Sina / Beijing Youth Daily, "周星驰回应'炒冷饭':每部新戏都会被说江郎才尽" (2019): reports he thought about `The New King of Comedy` for years, spent years getting the script ready, and used a different shooting method; notes he personally demonstrates acting for roles including extras, considers every role useful, values actors' contributions, and continues to focus on small people, effort, and dream-chasing.
  URL: https://ent.sina.cn/2019-02-15/detail-ihqfskcp5313322.d.html?from=wap

## Distillation Caveats

- Treat "周星驰式" as a set of reusable creative principles, not a claim of exact authorship.
- Separate observed source-backed habits from speculative psychology.
- Avoid copying dialogue, jokes, shots, or story structures from specific films unless the user explicitly asks for analysis or homage, and label homage clearly.
- If the user asks for commercial production advice, add practical constraints: budget, audience, censorship/regulatory needs, actor availability, shoot days, and post-production scope.
